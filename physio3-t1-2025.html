Question 1 – Acromegaly
Dave, a 50-year-old male, presents to the clinic extremely worried about some bodily changes his wife noticed. His hands have seemed to get larger as well as coarsening of his facial features.
a) What is the most likely cause of this condition? (1 mark)
Acromegaly.
b) On examination, you notice a loss of peripheral vision. What is this called and how is it caused? (2 marks)
Bitemporal hemianopia. It is caused by the enlargement of the pituitary gland (due to a GH-secreting adenoma) compressing the optic chiasm, which carries fibers from the nasal retinae that cross in the chiasm.
c) Give 2 complications of this condition? (2 marks)
Diabetes mellitus (due to GH-induced insulin resistance) and hypertension/cardiomyopathy. Other complications include sleep apnea, arthritis, and visual field defects.
d) What is the first line investigation? (2 marks)
Biochemical confirmation is via IGF-1 then later with GH measurement.
e) How reliable is measurement of blood GH levels in diagnosing GH-related disorders and why? (2 marks)
It is unreliable because GH is released in pulsatile bursts, especially during sleep, and its levels fluctuate throughout the day. A single random GH level is therefore not diagnostic. However, IGF-1 is a more reliable marker because it is produced in response to GH, has a long half-life, and its levels remain relatively stable throughout the day.
Question 2 – Hyperparathyroidism
A 30-year-old man presents with polyuria and polydipsia. He has severe colicky right groin pain as well. A CTKUB shows a right-sided renal calculus. His blood results show: eGFR >60 ml/min; Adjusted Ca²⁺ 3.1 mmol/l (2.1–2.6 mmol/l); phosphate 0.6 mmol/l (0.8–1.4 mmol/l); PTH 5.9 pmol (1.6–6.9 pmol/l).
a) Give four differential diagnoses. (4 marks)
1. Diabetes mellitus
2. Diabetes insipidus
3. Hyperparathyroidism
5. Addison's disease
b) What is the most likely diagnosis? (1 mark)
Hyperparathyroidism (primary). The elevated calcium with low phosphate and PTH in the upper-normal range is classic for primary hyperparathyroidism, though PTH is not fully suppressed. The presence of a renal calculus further supports this.
c) What is the most likely cause of this diagnosis? (1 mark)
A parathyroid adenoma (tumour of the parathyroid gland). In rare cases, it can be due to parathyroid hyperplasia or, even more rarely, a parathyroid carcinoma.
d) Give four more symptoms of this condition. (4 marks)
1. Fatigue and muscle weakness
2. Bone pain and pathological fractures
3. Constipation and abdominal pain
4. Depression and confusion
5. Nephrolithiasis (renal stones) — already present
6. Peptic ulcers
e) What is the definitive treatment? (1 mark)
Surgical removal of the parathyroid adenoma (parathyroidectomy). If surgery is not possible, medical management with cinacalcet, bisphosphonates, or calcimimetics may be used.
f) Briefly explain the physiological basis of renal stones in this individual? (1 mark)
It is caused by excessive precipitation of calcium with phosphates or oxalates in the kidneys due to hypercalciuria (high urinary calcium) resulting from the high serum calcium levels. The high calcium load filtered by the kidneys leads to supersaturation of urine with calcium salts, promoting stone formation.
Question 3 – Hypothyroidism
A 26-year-old woman presents with complaints of persistent fatigue, intolerance to cold weather, weight gain despite poor appetite, and recent heavy menstrual periods. On physical examination, her skin is dry, reflexes are delayed, and her thyroid is not enlarged.
a) What is the most likely endocrine disorder in this patient? (1 mark)
Primary hypothyroidism (likely Hashimoto's thyroiditis, given the absence of goiter).
b) Which organ is primarily affected in this condition? (1 mark)
The thyroid gland.
c) Explain the mechanism behind the patient's elevated TSH levels. (1 mark)
Due to decreased levels of T3 and T4, the negative feedback mechanism on the hypothalamus and anterior pituitary is lost. This increases the secretion of thyrotropin-releasing hormone (TRH) and thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH), leading to elevated TSH levels in an attempt to stimulate the failing thyroid gland.
d) What physiological role does thyroid hormone play in thermogenesis? (1 mark)
Thyroid hormones increase oxygen consumption and basal metabolic rate (BMR) by activating uncoupling proteins in mitochondria and stimulating the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase pump. This increases heat production (thermogenesis). In hypothyroidism, this is reduced, causing cold intolerance.
e) State one reason why the patient might be experiencing delayed reflexes. (1 mark)
Thyroid hormones increase synaptic transmission and myelination. In hypothyroidism, reduced levels of T3/T4 lead to decreased synaptic efficiency and delayed nerve conduction, manifesting as delayed deep tendon reflexes (hung-up reflexes).
f) Why is her menstrual bleeding likely to be heavier than usual? (1 mark)
Thyroid hormones normally reduce bleeding time and regulate menstrual cycles. In hypothyroidism, the lack of thyroid hormone leads to anovulatory cycles and prolonged, heavy menstrual bleeding (menorrhagia) due to impaired hemostasis and altered gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH) pulsatility.
g) What is the physiological explanation for her weight gain despite decreased caloric intake? (1 mark)
Thyroid hormones increase lipolysis and fatty acid oxidation. In hypothyroidism, decreased metabolic rate and reduced lipolysis lead to fat accumulation in adipose tissue, causing weight gain even with reduced caloric intake. Additionally, myxedema (mucopolysaccharide deposition) contributes to weight gain.
h) Mention two possible classes of drugs that could be used in managing the condition. Briefly explain their mechanisms of action (3 marks)
1. Levothyroxine (T4) – a synthetic form of thyroxine. It is converted to T3 in peripheral tissues and replaces the deficient thyroid hormone, restoring normal metabolic rate.
2. Liothyronine (T3) – a synthetic form of triiodothyronine. It acts more rapidly than T4 and is used in severe cases or myxedema coma.
i) Mention two effects of thyroid hormone deficiency on lipid metabolism? (2 marks)
1. Decreased lipolysis and fatty acid oxidation, leading to accumulation of triglycerides and weight gain.
2. Reduced LDL cholesterol clearance due to decreased synthesis of LDL receptors in the liver, leading to hypercholesterolemia and increased risk of atherosclerosis.
